# 01: Trade Management: Active vs Passive

## The Problem
A trader enters a position. It goes 2R in profit. The trader gets nervous, moves their stop to breakeven prematurely, gets wicked out, and then watches the trade run to their 5R target. Over-management destroys expectancy just as quickly as poor entries.

## The Story: The Pilot and Autopilot
A pilot takes off manually (the entry). Once at cruising altitude, they engage the autopilot and monitor the instruments. They don't aggressively yank the yoke left and right to avoid every small patch of turbulence. Active trade management should only occur when the core environmental parameters change, not at every 1-minute candle wiggle.

## The Concept
Trade management is the systematic process of reducing risk and securing profit as a trade progresses toward its target, based solely on structural market feedback.

### Why It Works
Passive management (set and forget) ensures you capture the full statistical edge of your system. Active management (scaling out, trailing) smooths the equity curve and protects capital, but often reduces overall R-multiples. You must choose a methodology and stick to it.

## How It Works
1. **Breakeven Mechanics:** Only move your stop to breakeven when the market has created a new structural high/low that defends your entry.
2. **Partial Exits:** Securing risk at key opposing liquidity pools while leaving a runner for the ultimate target.
3. **Time-Based Exits:** Closing trades before the weekend gap, or exiting a session trade if it hasn't hit target by the end of the kill zone.

## Key Takeaways
- **Once you are in a trade, you are a manager, not an analyst.**
- **Never move a stop loss further into negative territory.**
