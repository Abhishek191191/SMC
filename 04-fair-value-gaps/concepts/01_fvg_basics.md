# 01: Fair Value Gaps (FVG): The Imbalance

## The Problem
When the market moves slowly, buyers and sellers trade in a balanced, overlapping manner. But when news drops or institutions dump capital, the market moves so fast that one side of the order book completely disappears. Retail traders see a massive green candle and FOMO buy the top, completely unaware that the market has left an unstable vacuum behind.

## The Story: The Missing Rung on the Ladder
Imagine climbing a ladder, but one of the rungs is completely missing. You can step over it, but the ladder is structurally weak at that spot. Eventually, a carpenter has to come back and install that missing rung to make the ladder stable. An FVG is that missing rung in price delivery.

## The Concept
A Fair Value Gap (FVG), also known as an Imbalance, is a 3-candle sequence where the wicks of the 1st and 3rd candles do not overlap, leaving a gap in price where only buying (or only selling) occurred.

### Why It Works
The market is an efficient auction algorithm. It seeks balance. When price moves too quickly and leaves an FVG, it creates a pocket of inefficient pricing. The algorithm will naturally draw price back to this gap to offer fair trade to both buyers and sellers, filling the gap before continuing.

## How It Works
1. **The Impulse:** A massive institutional market order hits the tape.
2. **The Gap:** The 2nd candle is huge. The wick of the 1st candle and the wick of the 3rd candle do not touch.
3. **The Draw on Liquidity:** Price is magnetically drawn back to the FVG.
4. **The Fill:** Price enters the gap, balancing the books, and often uses it as a launchpad for the next move.

## Key Takeaways
- **FVGs act as both magnets and support/resistance.**
- **They are the ultimate confirmation of an Order Block.** An OB without a subsequent FVG lacks institutional displacement.
