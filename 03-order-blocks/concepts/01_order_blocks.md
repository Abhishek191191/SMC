# 01: Order Blocks: Institutional Footprints

## The Problem
Retail traders use moving averages and trendlines to find entries. These are lagging, mathematical derivatives of price. They don't represent actual orders in the market. Consequently, they enter late and place stops in random places.

## The Story: The Bank Vault
Imagine a massive bank vault. When the bank opens the vault and moves a billion dollars, they leave massive tracks in the mud outside. They can't hide it. An Order Block is that footprint. It is the exact location where a massive institutional order was injected into the market, so large that it caused a structural shift.

## The Concept
An Order Block (OB) is traditionally defined as the last opposing candle before a strong impulsive move that breaks structure.
- **Bullish OB:** The last down candle before an impulsive upward move.
- **Bearish OB:** The last up candle before an impulsive downward move.

### Why It Works
When institutions inject massive capital to reverse the market, they often have to scale in. Because the market moves away so violently (displacement), not all of their orders are filled. They leave limit orders at that origin point. When price eventually returns to this block, those limit orders act as a massive wall, causing price to bounce.

## How It Works: Mitigation
1. **Creation:** An institution buys heavily, causing a down candle followed by massive upward displacement. The OB is created.
2. **The Void:** Price rallies away, leaving unfilled orders at the OB.
3. **Mitigation:** Sometime later, price returns to the OB. The remaining institutional buy limit orders are triggered, and price rallies again.

## Key Takeaways
- **Volume matters.** An OB is only valid if it is followed by strong displacement (FVGs) and a Break of Structure.
- **It's an area, not a line.** The entire candle body (and sometimes the wick) represents the zone of institutional interest.
