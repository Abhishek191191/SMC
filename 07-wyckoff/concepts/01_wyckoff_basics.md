# 01: Wyckoff Method: The Anatomy of Accumulation

## The Problem
Many SMC traders get chopped up in sideways markets. They see a break of structure and take a trade, only for price to immediately reverse and break structure the other way. They are trying to trade a trend environment while the market is actually in a complex consolidation phase.

## The Story: Loading the Spring
Imagine trying to jump as high as you can. You don't just stand straight legged and hop. You have to squat down, compress your energy, and then explode upwards. The market does the same thing. A massive institutional trend doesn't just start; it requires a prolonged period of "squatting" (Accumulation) to build enough energy (orders) for the explosive move.

## The Concept
Richard Wyckoff observed that markets move in distinct phases: Accumulation, Markup, Distribution, and Markdown. Accumulation is the precise sequence of events where Composite Man (the institutions) quietly buys the asset from panicked retail traders before launching a bull market.

### Why It Works
Wyckoff schematics map perfectly to the psychology of the retail trader. The institution manipulates the price to induce fear (causing retail to sell at the bottom) and greed (causing retail to buy the top).

## How It Works: The Accumulation Schematic
1. **Selling Climax (SC):** Panic selling. Institutions start absorbing the massive sell orders.
2. **Automatic Rally (AR):** Sell pressure exhausts, price bounces.
3. **Secondary Test (ST):** Price tests the bottom to see if sellers remain.
4. **The Spring:** The ultimate manipulation. Price drops below the SC, triggering stop losses. Institutions grab the final liquidity.
5. **Sign of Strength (SOS):** Explosive move out of the range.

## Key Takeaways
- **The Spring is the key.** The move below the range low is not a breakdown; it is a liquidity sweep.
- **Wyckoff explains the "Why" behind SMC.** SMC provides the micro-entries (OBs, FVGs) inside the macro Wyckoff structure.
